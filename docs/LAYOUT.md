# The Layout

The layout says where everything stands: a box a node, the boxes of a layer side by side on a shelf with the lanes of the links that pass through it, and between two shelves a channel the links cross in. It is `graphlet/hooks/layout.ts`, and it is worked out for the room across a shelf alone, whatever window the graph is then seen through.

## Two Ways To Run

A graph runs `down`, its shelves rows of boxes one under another, or `right`, its shelves columns of boxes side by side. The layout is worked out the same way for both, across a shelf and along the layers, and everything below is said as it is running down: a place's `x` is how far across its shelf it stands, and a row is a step along the layers. Running right, across is down the room and along is across it, and the [picture](DRAWING.md#a-graph-that-runs-right) turns it, so its lines run the other way while every box and every word reads as it does running down. What a direction changes of the layout itself is this, the gap, the step between ports, and where a label lies held in `GRAINS`:

- **Boxes.** Running right, a box is as tall across its shelf as its label's lines and its border, and as long along the layers as its widest line; so a shelf is as long along as its widest box, and a narrower box stands at its start.
- **Gaps.** Two neighbors of a shelf are `gap` apart: two columns running down, one row running right.
- **Labels.** Running down, a link's label lies across the face it leaves, beside its port, so it takes room on the face. Running right, it lies along its line, on the line itself, just past its box, so it takes none, and a channel keeps as many columns past its shelf as its longest label takes, and one more.
- **The room.** Running right, the room across is the room's rows, less the foot's, and a graph is laid out across no more of them than its longest shelf takes, so its picture is as tall as it needs. Its layers are never folded.

## Boxes

A box says its node's mark, its label, and its badge. A node that links to itself carries the sign of a loop, `↺`, before its badge.

Widths are counted in cells, as a terminal draws text: `sizeOf` in `draw.ts` takes a character of an East Asian script, or an emoji, to be two cells, a mark on the character before it to be none, and any other character one, where a character is what a person sees as one, an emoji with all that is joined to it included. A label too long for one line is wrapped, by `wrap`, at its spaces, onto lines of up to `LABEL_COLUMNS` cells. A word a little longer than a line keeps a line to itself, so that a file's name is not broken for the sake of a few characters; a word longer than `WORD_COLUMNS` is broken after the last `/`, `-`, `_`, or `.` that fits a line, or with none, where the line ends. What is left after the last line a label may take, `LABEL_ROWS` of them, is cut short there, and ends in an ellipsis. With the `labels` setting at `clip`, a label keeps to one line and is cut in its middle to `CLIP_CELLS` instead, so that a file's name keeps how it starts and how it ends.

A box is as wide as its widest line and no narrower than `BOX_COLUMNS`, which leaves its two ports apart; a label shorter than that is set in the middle. A box is as tall as its own label, and a shelf as tall as its tallest box. A shorter box stands at the shelf's top, and what leaves or reaches its bottom runs on down past the rows it leaves.

## Ports

A link meets a lane in its one column. Each face of a box, its top and its bottom, has a port for each bundle of links that leaves it, and a port for each bundle that reaches it, the direction's `portStep` apart across its middle, so that the column or row between two is free to step a link aside at; the links with one label and one tone that leave a face of a directed graph's box are always one bundle. `dealPorts` orders a face's ports by where along their shelves the places at the links' other ends stand, so links from the left arrive on the left. Of two ports whose links lead to a place in common, the one with the flow is first, wherever their other links lead: so the two links between two boxes, one each way, stand in the same order on both boxes and run side by side. They may still cross where one leaves its box straight, or where the tracks the two turn on make them. A box too narrow for its ports is widened. So a link back up arrives beside the links that leave, and links from different boxes into one box arrive at arrowheads of their own, or in an undirected graph at ticks of their own.

A link's label, cut in its middle to `LINK_LABEL_CELLS`, is written beside the port it leaves by, along the face after it. So the port after a labeled one stands past the label and a gap, and a box is widened for its ports and labels together. Running right, it is written on its own line instead, just past its box, and the face makes no room for it. Links with different labels, or different tones, are never of one bundle, so every link that leaves by a port has the label written beside it, and a line is in the tone of every link on it. A link with a label always leaves by its own port, and a link that leaves straight keeps clear of the columns a label takes and of the column after it, so no line stands against a label. A label is written in the row just past the box its link leaves, which for a box shorter than its shelf is within the shelf. A link from a node to itself is drawn as `↺` in its box, and has no label written.

A link that crosses a channel leaves its box straight toward the port it is bound for, wherever the box reaches over that port clear of its corners and its other ports, and else by its own port. A link that leaves with others keeps clear of the columns beside their port, and every link that leaves straight keeps clear of the columns beside the face's other ports, so that it never runs beside another's tick or arrowhead.

## Shelves

`layoutOf` seats the longest shelf first, in the middle of the columns it is laid out across, and then each other shelf against the one nearer it:

- A box goes as near as it can to where its ports stand over or under the ports of the boxes it is joined to. The boxes are pushed apart where they would overlap, and slid back together toward where they wanted to be.
- A lane then goes to the very column of the port it is joined to, or the middle of those it is joined to, and pushes aside the boxes in its way, so that its links run straight through the shelves they pass.
- Last, the shelf moves a column either way where that stands more of it exactly over or under what it is joined to.

The shelves are then seated again, by `reseat`, `RESEATS` times over: each time, every shelf is balanced against the shelf above it, on the way down; against the shelf below it, on the way back up; and then against both, where the two are laid out across the same columns.

- Balancing a shelf seats its places where their wires to the shelves beside it run least sideways, all told, of the seatings that keep the places in their order, a gap apart, and within the shelf. Each place wants the median of the columns its wires would have it at, and places that would then stand out of order are pooled into one run at the median of all their wires; a place with no wires to them joins the run before it. Where a place's wires split evenly, it stands halfway between, so a box over two others stands centered over them.
- A lane's wires weigh `LANE_WEIGHT` times a box's, so that a link through lanes keeps straight, and a wire with the flow weighs `FLOW_WEIGHT` times one against it, so that the link that closes a loop bends before the links it runs back past.
- Where the graph's nodes have priorities, a wire is ranked by its link's: the higher priority of the link's two nodes. Where the wires are of more than one rank, the passes are made again, from the best seating so far, with only the wires of the highest rank a place has counting where it stands, so that a node of higher priority, and the places joined to it, stand by its links first. In those passes, a place pulled two ways, by a node of higher priority and one of lower, is balanced over the higher, and not between. A seating those passes make is kept instead of the seating kept so far only where its rows and bent links weigh no more, as below, and its bent links of each rank but the lowest weigh less, the highest rank first.
- Of the first seating and the seating after each pass made with no rank, the one kept is the one whose channels' rows and bent links weigh least, a row as much as `ROW_BENDS` bent links; a bent link with the flow between two boxes weighs one, one against the flow `FLOW_WEIGHT` times less, and a wire to or from a lane `LANE_BEND` times less again. Of those that weigh the same, the one kept is the one whose bent links of each rank but the lowest weigh least, the highest rank first, so that a graph with none, all of one rank, has no such choice; and then the one whose wires run least sideways, each cell of a wire weighing as its bend does. Since the passes balanced by rank come after the passes made with no priorities, which are what a graph with none is seated by, and their seatings never weigh more, a graph's priorities never leave its seating weighing more in rows and bent links than it would with none; where they change it, its links of higher priority bend less, though it may bend links of lower priority more, or take a row for the bends it saves.
- Last, where every shelf is laid out across the room's columns, the shelves are set together in the middle of them.

How wide a shelf is laid out is the `scroll` setting's to say. `pinned`: across the room's columns where it fits them, and where it is longer, across the longest shelf, together with every other shelf longer than the room, so that the long shelves lie on one sheet and their links run between them as laid out. `sheet`: every shelf across the longest.

## Frames

A group's frame is a box with round corners around every place of the group: its nodes, and the lanes of the links within it. `framesOf` stands it `FRAME_MARGIN` out from them across their shelves, its border and a blank inside it, and where the graph runs down, wide enough for the group's label in its top border, what it holds in its middle: the lines that cross that border, into the group's first shelf or out of it, are known by their ports, and where no stretch between two of them, or between one and a corner, holds the label, the frame is widened back past the first until one does. Along the layers, its top border takes a row of the channel over its first shelf, over the rows for arrowheads there, and its bottom border a row of the channel under its last shelf, under the rows for arrowheads and labels there; a group that starts on the first shelf has a row over all the shelves for it. Running right, where a row is a column, each takes a column and a blank one inside it, `FRAME_ENDS`.

A frame that would be wider than its room, as the group's shelves are seated, has each shelf's places of the group moved together first, to stand in the middle of all of them, so that the frame is as wide as its widest shelf and no wider; a graph folded into a reply counts each group's margins in the width of a layer it stands in, so that such a frame fits.

Nothing else stands inside a frame. After the shelves are seated, on each shelf a frame spans, what is not the group's is moved clear of it, a gap from its border: what stands before it in the shelf's order is moved back where it can be, and else the frame, with all it holds, is moved on; and what stands after it is moved on. That is done over and over, frames only ever moving on, until nothing moves; and where that leaves the layout reaching past its room, every shelf is packed from its start instead, each frame still whole with all it holds, and the whole set in the middle of the room, so that a reply's grouped graph fits where its layers do. A shelf a frame passes with nothing of its group on it takes the frame where it stands among its places. So a frame may push the layout wider than its room, and a graph with groups that is wider than its room is laid out on one sheet, every shelf across all of it, so that a frame stands at one place on every shelf it spans. A channel keeps a jog off the columns the sides of frames run down through it, `walls`.

## Channels

A channel is the rows between two shelves. The stretches of links that cross it are its wires, and so are the links between two nodes of the shelf above, which run under that shelf and back up to it. The last shelf has a channel under it only for those.

A wire that does not go straight across turns on one row of the channel, its track. A channel's rows are its `topRows`, for the arrowheads that point up and the labels of the links that leave the shelf above, when it has any, then a row a track, then its `bottomRows`, for the arrowheads that point down and the labels of the links that leave the shelf below, when it has any. Running down, either is one row; running right, as many as the longest label it holds takes, and one more. An undirected graph has no arrowheads, so its channels are their tracks, and the rows its labels need. A channel with no wires is one empty row.

### Tracks

Wires that share a track run into one line, which says that everything on it is joined. So wires share a track only as a bundle that says nothing false: the wires that leave one face, a fan, together with the fans of every other face whose wires reach the very same faces. Several boxes' links to one box alone are such a bundle, and so are two boxes that both link to the same two. A box's link into a lane that other wires reach too is a fan of its own, so that where it meets them at the lane, it does not join them to the box's other links.

A link with no direction is drawn with no arrowhead, so a line of such links has nothing to tell the boxes it leaves from the boxes it reaches, and a link with no direction is never in a bundle with one that runs one way. A link with no direction between two nodes of one shelf is a bundle alone, with a port of its own on each of its boxes. A line of links with no direction then joins each box whose bottom it meets to each box whose top it meets, or, meeting only the bottoms of two boxes, is the one link between them.

`layTracks` gives each bundle a track.

- Two bundles share a track only where their runs do not meet.
- A bundle that comes down a column to its track goes above a bundle that goes down the same column from its own, so that the two never run into one line.
- Where bundles ask that of each other in a ring, no order of tracks gives them all it. One of the ring is then cut in two at a jog: a column between where it must go under the bundle before it and where it must go over the bundle after it, that no wire of the channel comes down or up. Each half takes a track of its own and keeps the wire ends on its side, and a wire with an end on each side steps from the one to the other down the jog, crossing what lies between. A bundle is cut once at most. Where no bundle of a ring can be cut, one of the ring is not given its place, and the two share a stretch of that column; the other rings of the channel are cut the same way.
- Of the bundles that may go on a track, the ones that cross the rest least from there go first.
- Where the two shelves of a channel are laid out across different columns, they slide apart under a window, and what does not meet as laid out may once they have slid. There no two bundles share a track as laid out, and the channel keeps one row more, for a jog. Each time the window moves, the bundles are given their tracks again as the shelves now stand: bundles that do not meet share tracks again where that fits the rows the channel has, and else each has its own; and jogs are cut where rings now are, as many as there are rows for.

Wires of different bundles that cross are painted as one passing over the other, so that a junction always means joined.

With the `links` setting at `merged`, every wire of a channel with no label and no tone is one bundle with the others of its kind, on a track of its own: those that run one way, with the flow or against it, and those that run no way; and a face of a box has one port for each kind. That is shorter, and says less: which links are whose is told by selecting a node. Wires with a label or a tone are still bundled as they are kept apart, each bundle on a track of its own, so a label stays true of every link of the line beside it and a tone of every link of the line it paints, and such a link with no direction between two nodes of one shelf is still a bundle alone.
